# Consolidated user requirements

This file consolidates the user's requirements from the AI Personas design conversation through 9 October 2026. It is a single readable account of the requested outcome, architecture, examples, research, and delivery. Repeated requests are combined, and later clarifications control the meaning of earlier wording.

This is a statement of user intent, not a competing implementation manual. The authoritative chapters define the detailed responsibilities, boundaries, and failure paths; the existing requirement catalogue and evaluation criteria retain their identifiers and obligations. The local UR identifiers below provide an additional way to trace the user's requirements. They do not replace the protected invariants, requirement rows, or evaluation minima. A conflict must be resolved in the affected design, rather than bypassed by choosing one document over another.

## Product requirements

### Purpose and design scope

- **UR-001 Design before implementation.** Keep this repository design-only. Produce and refine an implementation-ready blueprint describing how the personas should operate. Do not add application code as part of this design work.
- **UR-002 One coherent design.** Review the whole design against these requirements. Refactor, correct, or extend every affected document so that the design gives a consistent account of the persona, its context, its actions, and its development. Consolidate or remove conflicting, redundant, or obsolete design material while preserving useful requirements and historical evidence. The goal is a robust, reliable design that can be implemented, rather than disconnected explanations of individual features.
- **UR-003 Address the actual need.** A persona must be able to respond to a human user's need and carry out the relevant work. Its characteristic way of working must remain meaningful during tasks, tool use, communication, coordination, collaboration, and self-directed activity.

### Persona identity and characteristic fragments

- **UR-004 Traits seed the persona.** Begin with a persona profile grounded in the established OCEAN trait vocabulary and VAD modeled-affect vocabulary. OCEAN covers Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism; VAD covers valence, arousal, and dominance or perceived control. Research whether additional traits are needed and justify any additions rather than assuming those two profiles are exhaustive or adding an arbitrary compulsory taxonomy.
- **UR-005 The initial LLM call authors the initial fragments.** Use the starting profile to produce the persona's initial characteristic fragments. These prompt parts must express the persona's own voice and character, providing the starting point for its subsequent development. Continuing fragment creation, revision, and self-harmonization must preserve that persona-specific characteristic meaning.
- **UR-006 Fragments embody character.** Characteristic fragments are persona-specific prompt parts. They must carry the persona's characteristic and behavioral meaning, rather than serve as generic instructions with a persona label attached. The selected fragments form the character-bearing part of the context supplied to the LLM.
- **UR-007 The LLM and selected context constitute the operating persona.** The LLM provides reasoning, while the selected characteristic fragments and relevant supporting context shape this persona's responses and decisions. Character must influence the way it acts, rather than exist only in a separate profile or biography.
- **UR-008 Character applies across behavior.** Show how characteristic fragments can influence actual task choices, tool and action use, communication, coordination, collaboration, and self-directed or self-dialogue activity. Different personas may favor different approaches, including more direct tool use or more collaboration. Do not reduce persona differences to writing style alone.
- **UR-009 Fragments are not memories or plain knowledge.** Use “fragment” to mean a characteristic prompt part, not an episodic memory, a general-purpose fact store, or plain domain knowledge. The persona is formed through these character-bearing prompt parts; it is not defined by a separate memory mechanism. Supporting histories and records can still be retained as assets.
- **UR-010 Do not introduce a separate learning mechanism.** Explain development through the ordinary LLM interactions that create, revise, organize, select, and, where permitted, remove fragments and assets. Do not add a distinct “learning” subsystem or relabel all stored information as learned persona character.

### Supporting assets and historical context

- **UR-011 Keep assets identifiable separately from characteristic fragments.** Plain domain knowledge, data, tools, skills, scripts, code, historical messages, records of actions and tool interactions, and other useful resources are assets or supporting context. They remain distinguishable from characteristic fragments even when the persona or LLM creates them. Plain assets need not be rewritten in the persona's voice. Authorship alone does not turn an asset into persona character.
- **UR-012 Retain useful resources for later work.** Provide for storing and organizing the useful tools, skills, knowledge, data, scripts, code, history, and other assets that the persona acquires or produces. Later work must be able to find relevant assets without reconstructing everything from the beginning.
- **UR-013 Give retained material an intended location and useful discovery cues.** When an LLM interaction proposes new or revised fragments or assets, it must also address where that material belongs and how it can be found again, including the intended file or path and relevant search or regex cues. Storage and discovery must be part of the design, rather than an unspecified step after generation.
- **UR-014 Compose context from the relevant kinds of material.** An LLM request may combine characteristic fragments with relevant tools, skills, domain knowledge, data, and historical interaction or action records. Preserve the distinction between the character-bearing prompt parts and the supporting material while assembling the context needed for the current decision.

### The ordinary LLM interaction

- **UR-015 Integrate work and self-maintenance in every interaction.** Every persona LLM interaction must provide for the current work, the disposition of fragments and assets, and the information needed for the next bounded context. This responsibility belongs in the request and is explicitly addressed in the valid response. This applies regardless of whether the immediate purpose is a task, communication, collaboration, coordination, tool use, or another persona action.
- **UR-016 Support the full range of appropriate maintenance.** The same interaction must be able to propose creating fragments, updating existing fragments, reorganizing them, and deleting them when permitted, together with corresponding maintenance of tools, skills, knowledge, data, history, and other assets. Maintenance must keep the persona's character and its useful resources effective and coherent.
- **UR-017 Consider maintenance without forcing a mutation.** The requirement to address maintenance on every interaction does not require a new fragment, a changed asset, a deletion, or a growing prompt on every response. Preserve appropriate existing material when no useful permitted change is warranted, with the explicit maintenance disposition required by the current design.
- **UR-018 Let the current interaction guide the next one.** The primary persona's current LLM interaction must determine which characteristic fragments and supporting resources are needed next, and provide the selection or discovery instructions that allow those items to be found. The next request uses the resulting bounded selection, and this process continues through the work.

### File based discovery and context efficiency

- **UR-019 Use ordinary files and searchable links.** Prefer simple file organization and search over unnecessary architectural complexity. Store and organize prompt fragments in files, including JSON where useful, with filesystem search, grep, and regular-expression cues for discovery. Fragments may carry cues that lead to other relevant fragments or assets, forming a navigable network without requiring the whole collection in context.
- **UR-020 Select resources through the same bounded discovery process.** Discovery must cover tools, skills, data, knowledge, history, and other assets as well as characteristic fragments. A fragment or current LLM result may indicate which resources to locate and which search cues to use.
- **UR-021 Select before loading the next model context.** Do not send a persona's hundreds of tools, its full catalogue, or its whole fragment and asset collection to the LLM or model provider so the model can filter it. Use the current request's need and selection instructions to retrieve only the relevant bounded information for a subsequent request.
- **UR-022 Make token and context cost a design concern.** Keep every LLM request context-efficient. Include enough relevant information to support good decisions while avoiding unnecessary prompt material and token cost. Research whether the design's proposed efficiency actually holds rather than treating selective retrieval alone as proof of lower total cost.
- **UR-023 Keep both the collection and the prompt compact.** Prevent fragments from growing indefinitely or becoming an inconsistent, bloated collection. Revise, combine, reorganize, and keep needed characteristic material so that the persona can navigate its resources, select the right fragments at the right time, and form compact, coherent context.
- **UR-024 Keep organization useful as the persona develops.** Resource organization and discovery cues must evolve along with the fragments and assets. The persona must be able to find useful earlier material after revision or reorganization, rather than merely accumulate files that are rarely used.

### Development and later usefulness

- **UR-025 Development arises through interaction.** Work, tool use, the environment, mistakes, communication with people, and interaction with other personas can shape subsequent fragments and assets. Explain how those ordinary interactions change the persona's characteristic behavior and future context without introducing a separate learning process.
- **UR-026 Maturation affects the whole persona.** Examine changes in characteristic behavior, affect, judgment, task performance, communication, coordination, collaboration, and other actions over time. The human-aging analogy describes useful development through experience and resource refinement; it is not satisfied by merely counting elapsed time or stored fragments.
- **UR-027 Aim for useful later work.** Maturation should make a persona more effective than its earlier stage, with useful improvements visible in behavior and work. These may include better prompting, choices, methods, coordination, and use of assets. Show what changed and how it could help later work. Do not assume that every change helps or that maturation inevitably improves every aspect of every task.
- **UR-028 Transfer selectively and reassess the new task.** Prior work may provide useful methods, simulation approaches, assets, judgment, or characteristic behavior in a later related task. Assess the new task on its own terms. Do not transfer an entire earlier domain solution wholesale or assume that prior experience makes the persona competent in every related domain.

### Worked examples and design verification

- **UR-029 Compare three personas over time.** Use three personas with distinguishable starting profiles and initial LLM-authored fragments. Show their initial state, relevant interactions, later fragments and assets, and subsequent behavior so that differences and maturation can be examined rather than merely described.
- **UR-030 Include the requested work cases.** Walk through designing a four-bedroom house and a five-watt DC-to-AC circuit. Identify missing project details as assumptions or open questions rather than inventing user requirements such as a circuit input voltage or frequency. Examine how the three personas' different characters affect their choices, collaboration, and work, and how those encounters may affect their later states.
- **UR-031 Include the later skyscraper case.** Give a later skyscraper-design task, potentially from a different human user, to a persona that participated in the house work. Show which earlier fragments, methods, tools, skills, or assets are selected, which are unsuitable, what still needs fresh investigation, and how any useful transfer affects the new task.
- **UR-032 Make the walkthroughs observable.** Provide step-by-step hypothetical request, response, action, and result or receipt walkthroughs. Show the chosen context, relevant resource discovery, work decisions, proposed fragment or asset changes, and the next-context selection. State assumptions and separate hypothetical outcomes from observations actually obtained.
- **UR-033 Examine benefit, harm, no effect, and missed benefit.** Check what prior development helps, what does not help, what harms a later task, and whether potentially useful material is missed or not applied. Investigate both domain-specific reuse and changes in general characteristic behavior, judgment, affect, or coordination.
- **UR-034 Use the examples to find and repair design gaps.** Audit the proposed design against the walkthroughs and all user requirements. Identify what works, what fails, missing behavior, contradictions, and unresolved questions; research them and refine the affected design rather than only appending examples or assurances.
- **UR-035 Distinguish a strong blueprint from proven effectiveness.** Pursue the user's goal of a robust and reliable design that will work when implemented. Do not claim that research, documentation, or hypothetical walkthroughs establish one-hundred-percent effectiveness, demonstrated persona benefit, or proven cost savings. Make the remaining evidence needed clear.

## Research and delivery requirements

These requirements govern the requested design assignment and its publication. They are not fixed schedules, runtime loops, or mandatory timing rules for the future persona system.

- **UR-036 Research thoroughly.** Conduct deep research on the current design and the user's requirements, including trait choices, fragment and asset organization, retrieval, context efficiency, behavior, maturation, and the gaps exposed by the worked examples.
- **UR-037 Use the latest requested research window and update cadence.** Earlier research requests specified five-hour periods and subsequently renewed ten-hour periods. For the current assignment, follow the latest request for a ten-hour research period with concise progress updates every ten minutes. This supersedes the earlier five-minute suggestion and the earlier five-or-ten-minute allowance for that assignment.
- **UR-038 Review against the whole requirement set.** Reflect on and audit the current GitHub design against all of the user's points. Check that changes remain coherent with the rest of the design and that no important requirement disappears during refactoring.
- **UR-039 Publish completed changes incrementally.** The earlier publication instruction established a new research branch in the ai-personas-design repository. Push completed, reviewed design changes to that existing GitHub remote research branch and continue the remaining work; a fresh branch is not required for every push. Do not wait for the end of the research period merely to publish work that is ready. Later push-now instructions reinforce this incremental delivery requirement.
- **UR-040 Consolidate the requirements in one file and publish it.** Rephrase all relevant human-authored requests as a deduplicated requirement set in one design document, retain later clarifications, and push that document to the remote branch. Keep the requirements understandable in this file; links may supply detailed design and evidence without replacing the necessary statement of intent.

## Historical investigation and runtime requests

These earlier requests are retained because the user asked for all messages to be represented. They describe separately scoped work, not present permission to add implementation code to this design-only repository. Their inclusion does not claim they were completed.

- **UR-041 Investigate and fix the reported runtime root causes.** The 1 October assignment requested GPT 6 Pro to examine the linked implementation and design branches, identify and fix the root causes of the reported house-run problems, establish GitHub write readiness before beginning, and push the changes. The investigation scope included interrupted-call and uncertain-exposure recovery, access to protected finishing allowance and accepted commitments, premature stopping in scope discovery, sharing identities and permissions, package validation, and delivery of generated artifacts. Those incident details were supplied as background in a quoted report; this consolidation does not independently verify the report or prescribe its diagnosis as a product requirement.
- **UR-042 Run the earlier model comparison.** The 4 October assignment requested actual AiPersonas runs of house-design and DC-to-AC circuit tasks using GPT 5.6 Sol and Luna, with observation of how the system worked and what issues arose. Keep those actual-runtime requests and their evidence separate from the later design-only, hypothetical three-persona program. These model choices belong to the historical assignments, not a compulsory model choice for the persona architecture.

## Clarifications that control interpretation

- “Fragments” means characteristic prompt parts. Historical memory terminology must not override the later, explicit distinction between those fragments and ordinary assets.
- Maturation means the development and effective use of fragments and separately maintained assets through ordinary LLM interactions. It does not require a separate learning mechanism.
- Every interaction addresses work, maintenance, and next context; useful permitted changes are conditional, not compulsory.
- House-to-skyscraper transfer is selective. The request expressly does not assume complete domain transfer or universal benefit.
- The request for complete reliability is a design objective. Hypothetical examples and research do not establish empirical success.
- The latest requested research cadence and incremental-push instructions govern this assignment, not the persona runtime.
