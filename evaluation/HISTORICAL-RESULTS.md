# Historical criteria, reported results, and transition mapping

## A changed design does not change an old result

An old result answers the question its original evaluation asked. Changing the design does not turn that result into a pass for the new design, erase a failure, or change what was tested.

The prior design baseline is [f4549bd594e6a0cf5205ac031ed422ab269def8d](https://github.com/ai-personas/ai-personas-design/tree/f4549bd594e6a0cf5205ac031ed422ab269def8d). Unless another revision is named, this page links earlier criteria to that fixed revision. Those links preserve history; they do not establish a second current specification. Readers do not need to study historical files to understand the current design.

**The current target.** The October 8, 2026 revision defines fragments as authored prompt parts that express the persona's character. Selected fragments provide context in which the primary LLM reasons, makes characteristic choices, acts and communicates. Experience can lead it to revise those prompts. Fragments are not a factual-memory store, and the persona is not merely a wording style: its context, reasoning, actual interactions and actions, and evolving authored prompts belong to the account.

The primary LLM chooses how to discover and select this context. It can use filesystem listings, literal or supported regular-expression searches, simple authored cues or indexes, and exact file reads. Persona-authored fragments use the persona's own first-person voice. The design requires one coherent, accepted, versioned character account, semantic OCEAN stable traits, and separate VAD modeled affect. OCEAN names openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness and neuroticism; VAD names valence, arousal and dominance. These are required semantic descriptions, not compulsory numeric scores. Every authored fragment must carry the owner's character, and every persona invocation must receive maintenance input. An accepted response must provide a valid maintenance outcome.

**What the new target replaces.** Required graph navigation, the former conditional graph-delegation contract, optional independent semantic selection, graph-specific structured disposition forms, and compulsory numeric character descriptors are superseded. The former Jev selector was optional under authorized delegation; it was never mandatory for every old run. Semantic, vector or graph discovery may be studied as explicitly different architectural comparisons. They are not conforming fallbacks for lexical fragment discovery. Ordinary task tools remain available within their own grants.

Maintenance consideration is again an explicit obligation. It does not require a new lesson, edit, separate reflection call or self-rewrite. The earlier file proposal's strict on-demand-only rule, which prohibited persistent selection, is also superseded: a primary-authored selection may persist within its work scope under the current contract.

The mappings below use three different dispositions:

| Disposition | What it means |
|---|---|
| Carried concern | An underlying obligation about authority, continuity, usability, evidence or behavior remains in the current core criteria. Its old graph- or provider-specific test setup does not become mandatory for every implementation. |
| Superseded architecture | An old mechanism is no longer required for conformance. Its original tests and outcomes remain history for that mechanism. They are neither converted into current passes nor used to erase failures. |
| Retained campaign | A historical behavioral task or comparison keeps its original rubric, controls, minimum counts, failures and intervention record. Changing its setup requires a new evaluator revision. |

The 21 invariants and 45 requirement identifiers remain active. M01–M26, B01–B12 and X01–X06 remain the active catalogue, with explicit revisions for files and context. Supplemental identifiers in the tables provide traceability. They do not add runtime objects or another required catalogue. Similar labels do not establish equivalent tested behavior.

## October 8 applicability and the maintenance change

The October 8 change made character descriptions and explicit maintenance outcomes mandatory. The immediately preceding file-first wording had made OCEAN and VAD optional and allowed ordinary no-edit responses to omit a maintenance disposition. That wording no longer defines the target.

OCEAN and VAD now have required, distinct semantic roles without required scores. A profile and a current-self representation must express the same accepted character. Numeric values in historical campaigns remain a record of what those campaigns actually supplied.

**Every invocation and accepted response.** The current maintenance obligation applies throughout persona work:

- Every dispatched persona-model invocation receives maintenance input. This includes initialization, context selection, tool follow-up, peer coordination, retries, repair and final answers.
- Alongside its work, an accepted response provides a proposed patch, a reasoned no-change decision, or a bounded blocked/deferred outcome. As clarified later on October 8, maintenance must explicitly cover both persona-defining fragments and supporting assets.
- Missing or malformed maintenance invalidates the response and blocks substantive dispatch. Independent host safety and accounting still continue.
- A failed or refused generation keeps its actual failure status. The host must not fill the gap with an invented persona-authored no-change decision.

This obligation does not restore graph machinery or create another reflection author. Restricted selection repair remains edit-free and supplies a maintenance outcome limited to that mode.

**Next-context decisions changed again on 10 October.** At the October 8 revision, a response could omit a next-context update and preserve a still-valid choice. The 10 October explicit-intent correction supersedes that policy: every accepted response now states its next-context disposition for both fragments and resources. Earlier results that allowed omission receive no retrospective pass under this rule.

A genuine effective-character change also keeps the existing fresh-decision fence: all fresh operations, including reads and waits, remain subject to it. Ordinary method corrections and transient VAD updates do not automatically trigger that fence.

**What the revised evaluation examines.** The October 8 evaluation adds or makes explicit:

- Coverage of every invocation; coherent meaning across traits, profiles and current-self representations; own voice in every authored fragment; and separation of stable traits from transient state.
- Distinct action, coordination and learning channels.
- Neutralization controls and ablations, which remove selected influences to test what difference they make.
- Harmful transfer, resistance to pressure, correction, adaptive convergence and whole-system cost.

A historical pass does not cover these revised claims merely because its identifier remains in use. Historical comparisons, failures, original criteria, minimum counts and denominators stay unchanged. New numerical thresholds proposed by research are optional campaign choices. They must be fixed before execution; they are neither inherited results nor universal requirements.

**Reciprocal development.** The same revision makes a compact collection of cue-connected prompt fragments explicit. Character shapes work and communication with humans and peers. Actual consequences can support changes to authored prompt meaning, later choices and, where permitted, current character. Character changes remain subject to the existing ordering and fresh-decision rules.

The proposed comparisons separate three questions:

- What changes when personas start with the same character but have different histories?
- What changes when personas have different characters but matched experience?
- What difference do authored query cues make compared with no-cue or exact-reference navigation?

These comparisons clarify B02–B04, B07, B09–B10 and B12. They report no observed maturation, learning benefit or universal law of aging. Revision may preserve existing prompts or produce harmful change; revision alone is not improvement.

**Bounds and difficult cases.** Expanded boundedness cases count cumulative navigation effort and the whole declared managed fragment/evidence footprint. That footprint includes archives, versions, indexes, provenance, qualifications, warnings, caches and the peak temporary copies created during rewriting.

Tests under existing mechanical identifiers now explicitly cover external storage accounting limits; permitted raw-source expiry as distinct from revocation; preservation of rare exceptions and unresolved obligations; restored retired guidance; and cue injection. A truthful capacity block can satisfy a boundary while still failing to deliver the work. Silently losing required state cannot.

These refinements introduce no new identifier, universal storage size or passing result. The three-seed, three-task/three-repetition transfer and three-fresh-environment house minima retain their exact existing scopes.

### Prompt-part clarification and the limit of memory benchmarks

Remembering a fact is different from expressing a persona's characteristic judgment. Earlier file-first prose sometimes treated retained memory or successful recall as the defining purpose of fragments. The October 8 clarification replaces that framing with persona-defining prompt context and its evolution.

Factual histories, receipts and evidence remain separately governed supporting records. They are available when a claim needs them, but preserving or retrieving those records does not by itself instantiate the persona. Current evaluation must examine how selected prompt meaning changes characteristic judgment and behavior while factual information remains comparable. Reproducing an old fact is not enough.

Historical memory, recall and retrieval benchmarks remain useful only as limited adjacent evidence. They may support claims about discovery, access, storage or experience-conditioned behavior in the exact setting tested. Their results, failures and criteria do not change. They do not establish this design's account of what a persona is, the characteristic influence of selected context, or meaningful evolution of prompt parts.

Historical character and transfer findings likewise retain their precise intervention and scope. A new prompt-part comparison cannot inherit their pass. Legacy MEM and FR identifiers preserve traceability; they do not require fragments to be defined as memory.

### Seed bootstrap and bounded supporting-resource context

The October 8 refinement makes the normal beginning explicit. A persona starts from an attributed trait seed, accepts a coherent initial account, and continues revising through its primary LLM. The sequence is:

1. Supply the trait seed and separate modeled state.
2. Use initialLLM to author a bounded set of candidate persona fragments in the persona's own voice.
3. Accept a coherent starting account and hand it over to continuing primary authorship.
4. Continue primaryLLM self-harmonization and later evolution shaped by evidence.

InitialLLM names a bounded role. It may use the same model configuration as the continuing persona; it is not another standing writer. Seed fidelity, initial phrasing, coherent acceptance, actual observation and useful later change are separate claims. A different supplied origin must keep its actual attribution; supplied text is not retroactively generated text.

Proposed comparisons separately examine the order of the whole prompt bundle, redundancy, conditional scope and correction across calls. Exact inclusion of text does not by itself establish useful composition. Harmonization must preserve meaningful tensions and qualified disagreement. It is neither forced consensus nor proof of an experienced history. Historical runs keep the seed, prompt and initialization procedure they actually used, with no retrospective bootstrap or harmonization pass.

**Supporting resources remain distinct.** Histories and evidence, tool definitions, skill instructions, helpers and scripts, and task data are supporting resources. They are not new kinds of persona-defining fragment. Domain knowledge, generic procedures, code and other assets likewise retain their supporting purpose and attribution regardless of authorship or first-person styling.

The current refinements test primary-requested bounded lexical discovery or exact access to a known resource, explicit next-context choice, and actual exposure in the request sent to the provider. Loading hundreds of catalogue names and descriptions into the model and then asking it to filter them is not the target. A host's catalogue, the provider-facing payload and model-visible context are distinct: a bulk host catalogue must not become either exposure surface merely to support filtering. An exact known-current resource can take the fast path without compulsory rediscovery.

Cache validity and freshness, changed revisions, withdrawal, revocation, eviction, cumulative exposure and hidden schema expansion all require current evidence.

**What the comparisons must retain.** The proposed comparisons keep M01–M26, B01–B12 and X01–X06, controls for matched factual information and characteristic influence, all existing minima, and complete denominators for failures and interventions. They assess discovery quality, misses of critical capabilities, actual prompt exposure and adequate delivered work separately, against total cost per call and across the episode.

A full-catalogue condition is a labeled experimental comparator, not current production behavior. External deferred-loading results motivate the comparison but cannot supply its outcome. No historical selector, file, tool, cost or live-pilot result establishes these expanded claims. The documentation refinement produces no runtime code, experiment, paid model call, publication or deployment approval.

**Both maintenance scopes and both next-context scopes.** A later October 8 clarification required every accepted response to cover maintenance of both persona-defining fragments and supporting assets, alongside work and the bounded next-context selection affordance. That historical clarification still allowed omission to preserve a valid selection. The 10 October correction instead requires explicit next-context judgment for fragments and resources. It requires neither a fixed preserve field nor another call. An explicit preserve/no optional need decision can suffice without a new search. Recovery of stored state after a failure remains distinct from an authored choice.

The resulting boundaries are concrete:

- Proposed creation, storage, revision, organization or permitted deletion identifies the intended files or paths and useful future discovery cues. An explicit shared no-change reason is valid when it clearly covers fragments and assets.
- Proposed bytes or a pathname do not prove that a revision was actually stored.
- Authored histories cannot invent host observations or receipts.
- Restricted selection repair gains no authority to write semantic asset content.
- Missing required coverage blocks fresh substantive dispatch under the existing invalid-response boundary.

These are additional current cases under existing IDs. They are not evidence that old runs supplied or passed the expanded response contract. The historical outcomes below remain unchanged.

## New evidence must name the new claim

A future result must identify exactly what was evaluated. It must record the current design, runtime and evaluator revisions; enabled mechanisms; fixed criteria; source evidence; failures and interventions; total resources; and gates that were not run.

A compatibility file view over old records demonstrates only what was actually exercised. Hiding graph controls, rewriting prose or passing a local document check cannot establish any of the following:

- Characteristic primary-LLM behavior under selected persona-defining prompt context, or meaningful evolution of those fragments.
- Autonomous compact organization or bounded navigation through authored cues.
- Semantic separation of traits and state, or coherent character representations.
- Valid maintenance across every persona invocation.
- Qualified transfer, bounded adaptation or useful task delivery.

Retrieval of supporting factual history needs its own evidence and retains its own limited claim.
