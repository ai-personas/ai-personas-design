# Persona-owned process, not assigned roles

This normative clarification refines the existing no-fixed-roles, cooperation,
action and evidence requirements. It does not add a task router, another runtime,
a profession registry or a new requirement family. It distinguishes intended
behavior from any implementation or validation claim.

## Operations are possibilities

The persona owns the decision to process a request, create a result, examine it,
consult, assess, revise, pursue an alternative, continue or stop. These are
activities available to the same continuing identity, not classes into which the
system sorts personas. An optional operation must not become a mandatory phase
because a request has a certain domain, size, file type or number of failures.

A persona may propose or reuse a plan, checklist or procedure. Other participants
can adopt it within their authority, question it or propose alternatives. That
plan remains authored work content. It must not silently become a runtime rule
for all similar tasks or an assignment of another persona's responsibilities.

There is no required author/reviewer split, committee, review count, research
step, tool probe, successful shell command, repair loop or scope-coverage stage
for every task. Useful checking can be extensive when the actual claim warrants
it. Personas make that substantive judgment; an explicitly accepted user
requirement is not erased by making the available process role-neutral.

## Assessment does not assign a profession

Any participating persona with access to the subject may offer an assessment,
including of its own work. A persona examining another contribution may create
and submit an alternative in the same participation. Recording a judgment must
not automatically put it into waiting, close its responsibility, prescribe a
repair or prohibit further creation.

An assessment has an unambiguous subject and actual attribution. A direct
assessment can use an explicitly selected submission. Choosing a focus for
self-reflection need not create another persona or another run. A request for a
peer perspective is an invitation that the peer can accept, decline, discuss or
answer in a different useful way; it is not an installed reviewer role.

A legacy name such as reviewer or review_of can identify the actual assessor or
subject. It must not confer an activity monopoly. Access-scoped participation and
contributor-roster facts remain separate from activity choices; removing a role
gate must not silently grant access to private material or change who accepted an
existing agreement.

## Judgment, evidence and approval are different

A self-assessment records the author's interpretation. It is not independent
approval. A peer judgment is not human consent or external certification. A
separate identity is not proof of competence or meaningful independence.

A formal evidence binding can claim qualifications only when its exact subject,
observations, applicable scope, chosen independence conditions and accepted
requirements support them. Permitting a persona to record a perspective does not
assert those qualifications. Missing current scope is an explicit limitation,
not a reason to forbid exploratory thought or fabricate a formal review pass.

Additional citations may be empty. When supplied, they identify real accessible
receipts with their actual state and limitations. A failed operation may be
relevant; a successful one may not be. Recording a citation does not establish
that it was included in a prior model request or understood correctly.

## Criticism informs rather than dictates

A rejected or incomplete assessment stays visible and attributable. By default
it is advice, not a runtime-created veto. The software must not infer a required
repair, different tool, additional reviewer, repeated attempt or stop merely from
that verdict. Personas decide what the observation means for their work.

A blocking obligation is a separate explicit decision, associated with its
subject and accepted responsibility or scope. It can arise from a participant's
judgment or an already adopted requirement, not a hidden universal classifier.
Once explicitly accepted, its current status and disposition cannot be erased by
acknowledgment, compaction or a software upgrade. Disagreement remains possible;
it is not converted into agreement by averaging verdicts.

Existing historical blockers retain their recorded meaning until explicitly
disposed. New advisory behavior is not retrospective permission to waive an
accepted obligation. Replaying advice must not downgrade a later explicit block.

## Host execution is not a workflow sandbox

The ordinary local CLI experience should permit host tools for a newly created
workspace without an application sandbox or per-tool execution grant. Tool
selection, installation, filesystem work, network use and subprocesses then use
the host account's real permissions. The persona chooses the means within the
work and authority actually entrusted to it.

Existing deployments retain their recorded execution choice unless the operator
explicitly changes it. A deployment that the operator deliberately isolates must
identify that choice and its limitations; it must not silently fall back to
broader access after an isolation failure. A persisted host-tools choice is not
an unrestricted test profile and does not falsify resource accounting.

Host access does not provide confidentiality between personas sharing an account.
Application record permissions cannot protect credentials or files that the same
account can read. The interface must say so. Configuration changes and stop
receipts must not promise rollback of completed external effects or containment
of every already-started subprocess.

Authentication, explicit user permissions, actual funding limits, withdrawal,
pause/cancel, durable attribution and reference integrity are not persona job
roles. They remain infrastructure boundaries, not claims that the software knows
which substantive next step is correct. Transport limits and fair scheduling
must be described separately from behavioral autonomy.

## Observable acceptance cases

A conformance evaluation should demonstrate a persona creating a contribution
while considering a peer's submission; self-assessment in its existing
participation without a claim of independent approval; and direct assessment of
one chosen subject without a reviewer assignment. Missing or ambiguous subjects
must produce a request for a definite reference, not hidden selection by a task
router.

A negative assessment should preserve the finding without changing the author's
activity status or installing a blocker. A separate explicit blocking decision
must remain effective. Receipt replay, recovery and current permission changes
must preserve those distinctions.

For host execution, evaluate a new workspace's default, preservation of an
existing operator choice and an explicit switch. Verify the actual execution
mode instead of inferring it from prompt text. A normal funding/accounting
profile must not be replaced by a test profile to obtain host tools.

These are acceptance cases for the implementation, not a checklist imposed on
personas doing ordinary work. A written test, passing record-integrity fixture,
or accurately stored verdict does not establish substantive task quality or
reliable autonomous judgment. Report source changes, compiled tests, live
provider behavior and end-to-end outcomes separately and tie evidence to exact
versions.

The required compact learning-and-context handoff describes each persona's choice;
it does not require a fragment, a reflection call, a research phase or a repair
loop. No-change, deferral, independent continuation and an explicit partial result
remain possible. Search is primary when unfamiliar external requirements or tool
suitability need evidence, not a ritual when current applicable evidence is
already available. UI views distinguish authored focus, private learning choices,
optional human contributions and actual requests for human authority. They must
not infer an agenda or competence from a trait score or retained-fragment count.
