# 6. Evidence, review, and honest completion

[Design index](README.md) · [Previous: authority](05-authority-and-resources.md) · [Next: human experience](07-experience-and-society.md)

## The central rule

A claim must be no stronger than its evidence. “The tool is installed,” “the operation finished,” “the output is intact,” “the result meets the criterion,” and “the person accepted it” are different claims.

The evidence chain connects an accepted need and commitment to exact inputs and assumptions, an actual action or observation, exact outputs, an appropriate assessment, and a current applicability decision. The final result states its limits.

A simple conversation may have one response as its artifact and human judgment as its assessment. No tool run or numerical score is required merely to fill every box in a diagram.

## Artifacts, submissions, and assemblies

An artifact version identifies its exact content, producer or producing action, work scope, input versions, type, access policy, and derivation links. A native source, exported view, and preview are not interchangeable evidence.

A logical current result points to an adopted version without erasing earlier ones. A submission seals the exact artifacts, criteria, assumptions, limitations, and assembly relationships offered for review. “Whatever is latest” is not an adequate submission reference.

An assembly is the exact collection of versions intended to work together. Individually acceptable outputs may still be inconsistent as a combined result. Coordinated adoption must prevent a half-updated assembly from appearing current.

A content fingerprint can establish unchanged bytes. It does not establish authorship, correctness, consent, or approval without the corresponding records.

## Analysis and observation

A claimed calculation, simulation, or experiment preserves the question, criterion, source versions, transformation into inputs, relevant units and conventions, method and tool configuration, parameters, assumptions, actual execution or observation, warnings, results, interpretation, and limitations.

A successful conversion does not prove that analysis inputs faithfully represent the source. A successful process does not prove that the method was appropriate. A generated image is not a physical measurement.

Distinguish simulation, an outside participant's report, and independently observed measurement. Missing input mappings, unavailable visual access, unrun analysis, and invented artifact references cannot satisfy the corresponding evidence obligations.

## Review is accepted work, not a request count

A review identifies its exact claim, criteria, submission, reviewer, accepted responsibility, funding, independence policy, conflicts, actual checks, findings, verdict, and limitations. The review should check both listed requirements and material omissions from the original mandate.

Separate identity, separate execution, a different model, and qualified external assessment are different kinds of separation. A newborn is not automatically an independent reviewer of its parent's output. The required separation depends on the claim and must be chosen explicitly.

If no suitable reviewer accepts, the state is review unavailable or incomplete. A file-integrity check does not substitute for an engineering check. A human can accept a subjective creative result without inventing a numerical quality score, but human acceptance does not create missing technical evidence.

A blocking finding stays open until its adopted disposition is supported. Acknowledgment is not repair. Authorized deferral is possible only where the applicable criterion and authority allow it; it cannot convert an unmet mandatory requirement into an unconditional pass.

## Historical verdict versus current applicability

The review itself is historical: it records what was checked and concluded at that time. Its applicability to a current claim is separately derived and may be current, stale, pending, or unverifiable.

Changing a relevant input, assumption, criterion, tool or check configuration, assembly member, or review policy requires a new applicability decision. A review of version 3 remains a review of version 3 after version 4 exists.

Affected current claims must show stale or revalidation-pending status immediately, not remain green while background invalidation waits. An old result can remain inspectable and valid for its old conditions without supporting a new claim.

Dependency links may be incomplete. When impact is uncertain, require broader revalidation—up to the whole assembly—until a narrower scope is justified. The absence of a recorded dependency is not proof that no dependency exists.

## A release binds one coherent state

Final release binds the exact mandate, criteria, assumptions, submitted assembly, review policy, qualifying assessments, blocker dispositions, current authority, and delivered scope in one coherent accepted transition.

Consider a review of package 7 followed by a relevant input change. If the input change is accepted first, the old review cannot seal a current release. If release is accepted first, package 7 remains a historical release and the change creates a new candidate. The system must never show a new artifact combined with an old pass as a result nobody actually reviewed.

Historical human acceptance stays attached to the result accepted. It does not move silently to later versions. A new finding may affect what can currently be claimed without rewriting the fact that an earlier release occurred.

## Completion has several axes

| Axis | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Activity | Is work running, waiting, paused, or stopped? |
| Responsibility | Who accepted continuation and each required outcome? |
| Coverage | Which adopted outcomes have evidence, and has omitted scope been checked? |
| Submission | Which exact candidate is available? |
| Validation | Which checks passed, failed, or remain incomplete? |
| Applicability | Do those checks apply to the current candidate and conditions? |
| Human acceptance | Did the relevant person accept this exact result where required? |
| Outside validation | Which professional, site, external, or physical conditions remain unresolved? |
| Optional improvement | What further work is proposed without blocking the accepted milestone? |

A fraction must state its denominator: “Five of seven adopted outcomes have current evidence; scope coverage review is pending” is more informative than “85% complete.” An ended activity episode is not a completed need.

## Honest outcome vocabulary

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Delivered | The agreed scoped result was delivered with required current evidence. |
| Delivered with conditions | An authorized conditional result was delivered; its conditions remain visible. |
| Partial delivered | Useful portions were delivered, but the original full need remains unmet. |
| Blocked internally | A capability, ownership, integration, or resource gap prevents completion. |
| Blocked externally | Required information, permission, review, or outside observation is unavailable. |
| Unaccepted | A result exists, but the required acceptance is absent or declined. |
| Canceled or declined | Work ended by an explicit disposition rather than implied success. |

The conditions of a limited result must be visible beside the claim. An approved scenario assumption cannot support an unconditional real-world result. A coordinated digital design is not automatically permission to construct, operate, treat, publish, or certify anything.

## What the user receives

A release or stopped-state report identifies the accepted need, exact deliverables, applicable checks, unresolved assumptions, missing scope, outside requirements, remaining owners or ownership gaps, resource usage and uncertainty, and what decision would be needed to continue.

Optional improvements are distinguished from required repair. Personas may retain permitted lessons and continue as identities, but the completed work does not automatically renew their permissions or resources.
