# 7. Human experience, communities, and ongoing services

[Design index](README.md) · [Previous: evidence](06-evidence-and-completion.md) · [Visual guide](../VISUAL-GUIDE.md)

## The experience should answer six questions

What are we trying to achieve? What is happening now? Who accepted what? What changed? What evidence supports the current result? What needs my decision?

Primary navigation should use understandable concepts: Work, Personas, Environments, Learning, and Tools. Connections, resources, governance, and advanced networking appear where relevant rather than becoming prerequisites for every small request.

| View | What a reader should find |
|---|---|
| Work overview | Original need, accepted scope, current disposition, resources, blockers, and decisions needed from a human. |
| Perspectives | Attributed shared proposals, concerns, and differences—not a claim to reveal everyone's private thoughts. |
| Outcomes and commitments | Required outcomes, offered versus accepted responsibilities, dependencies, ownership gaps, and evidence. |
| Participants and agreements | Membership, actual endorsements, dissent, temporary delegations, and birth provenance. |
| Artifacts and evidence | Exact sources, previews, completed analyses, receipts, review, and current versus historical applicability. |
| Decisions and learning | Scope changes, feedback dispositions, handoffs, revised fragments, and later-use evidence. |

A persona profile separates authored character, interests, demonstrated capability, current commitments, and lifecycle. Model details can be secondary. An avatar animation is not evidence of work, competence, or emotion. A claimed generated portrait must correspond to a real artifact; otherwise show an explicit placeholder.

## Human control must be meaningful

People must be able to inspect constraints, change them within their authority, approve or decline scope changes, allocate resources, inspect birth and recruitment, approve accurately scoped effects, pause or cancel, request review, and accept limited results with visible conditions.

Approval requests must describe consequences, material assumptions, destination or audience, relevant content, limits, and alternatives. Do not repeatedly pressure a person to expand access simply to keep personas busy.

Present stop requested, actually stopped, effect already occurred, and effect unknown separately. Revocation stops new admissions, not history. The user should be able to inspect an old result without mistaking it for current acceptance.

## Accessibility and empty or failure states

The interface should support keyboard use, clear focus, readable text, small screens, and progressive disclosure. Status must not depend on color, animation, or an image alone. Every design diagram in this repository has a text explanation.

An artifact viewer distinguishes connecting, receiving, verifying, preparing, ready, unavailable, and failed. Receiving all bytes is not proof that a preview is ready or that a result is valid. Closing the viewer should release unnecessary work and resources.

An empty installation should explain how to create or select founders rather than display a fictional active society. No accepted continuation owner, unavailable tool, declined invitation, exhausted allowance, missing review, and stale result each need a visible explanation and an appropriate next decision. Do not replace these with a generic error or a misleading success badge.

## A society is not a collective mind

The broader community model is proposed extension E2. A society is a continuing governed network of people, personas, environments, agreements, resources, and work. A team is a temporary arrangement around outcomes. An institution is a continuing arrangement with a mandate, participants, authority, and review obligations.

These are descriptions of relationships, not mandatory new software engines. A society need not have one leader, culture, universal priority list, or global memory. Private information is shared only through explicit access rules.

Before managing consequential shared work or resources, a community should adopt a charter covering purpose, accountable human or institutional sponsor, membership and exit, actual decision authority, representation, resource allocation, information policy, conflicts, appeal, renewal, and dissolution.

## Real representation and scoped decisions

A community record distinguishes the requester, authorized decision-maker, beneficiaries, affected parties, actual consulted people, and missing representation. A persona may analyze a hypothetical viewpoint, but simulated residents cannot supply actual residents' consent.

A collective decision identifies the exact question, eligible participants, adopted decision rule, real endorsements, dissent, controlling authority, and resulting commitments. A vote cannot establish empirical truth or grant powers the voters do not possess. Creating more personas cannot increase a human-controlled voting or spending allocation.

Reputation should be a contextual portfolio of inspected contributions and limitations, not one universal popularity score. A well-regarded author still needs claim-specific review. Disagreement with a powerful participant is not automatically bad character.

Complaints and contested decisions need an adopted appeal route with an identifiable accountable human or institution. Departure or dissolution must leave every obligation handed off, canceled with authority, or visibly blocked; it must not trigger unrestricted copying of private memories.

## Sensitive human-facing uses

Proposed extension E3 covers transparency and safeguards for personal support, education, and public participation. The persona's AI nature should be clear. People should be able to decline memory or leave. Do not invent relationships, human experiences, diagnoses, qualifications, or independent human endorsements.

A useful conversation need not become a scored optimization task. A learner's temporary difficulty need not become a permanent ability label. Any sensitive deployment needs an appropriate policy and evaluation for its particular use; this handbook does not establish suitability merely by listing an example.

## Ongoing services use bounded episodes

Expanded service behavior is proposed extension E4. A continuing service has a mandate, accepted owner, permitted triggers, response bounds, observation-freshness rules, resources per episode and overall, review and renewal conditions, escalation, retention, and a visible stopping mechanism.

Completing one episode does not establish success for every future episode. An inactive or disconnected service must disclose its observation gap, not claim continuous monitoring. Expired triggers must not silently renew themselves.

A human escalation route must identify an actual recipient, accepted responsibility, acknowledgment expectations, and what happens if nobody responds. “Ask a human” is not sufficient when no reachable human has accepted the handoff. Missing essential escalation requires a narrower operation or a safe pause under the adopted policy.

## Physical interaction is a separately gated extension

Digital embodiment does not require a robot. A physical interface is an additional capability with stronger requirements: specific device identity, permitted location and actions, reliable relevant observations, limits, human override, safe stopping, delayed-effect handling, and evidence required before authority expands.

Observation loss, uncertain execution, and conflicting controllers must have explicit responses. Simulations do not establish physical safety. The deployment must obtain the particular external and qualified assessment its use requires; this handbook is not a certified control design or permission to operate machinery.

Cross-host persona activation is also not implied by community diagrams. Local continuity and read-only exchange can be useful without claiming globally exclusive identity control. Expanded federation needs separate transfer, revocation, authority, privacy, and failure contracts.
