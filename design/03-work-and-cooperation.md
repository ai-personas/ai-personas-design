# 3. Human needs, accepted work, and cooperation

[Design index](README.md) · [Previous: learning](02-memory-and-learning.md) · [Next: action](04-capabilities-and-action.md)

## Begin with the need, not a profession roster

A person can start in ordinary language. The system preserves the exact request, its origin, supplied material, and authorized boundaries. It does not require a large form before a sentence rewrite, and it does not route a house request into predetermined professional personas.

As consequences and dependencies grow, the **mandate** becomes more explicit: required outcomes, hard constraints, preferences, accepted clarifications, unknowns, assumptions, permissions, resource limits, review expectations, and stopping conditions. The depth of documentation follows the work, not a fixed ritual.

| Meaning | Example | Who may change it? |
|---|---|---|
| Original intent | The person asked for four bedrooms. | The person or an explicit delegate. |
| Adopted interpretation | The agreed result includes coordinated services. | Participants within their delegation; material changes need appropriate approval. |
| Optional improvement | Another arrangement may simplify maintenance. | Anyone may propose; adoption requires scope and resource authority. |
| Completion criterion | Editable sources and specified current checks are required. | The authorized party adopts a new version; earlier results remain historical. |

A useful negative result, focused question, partial delivery, or justified decline is valid when the full need cannot be met. None permits quietly removing required scope to claim success.

## Someone must accept continuation

Selecting a persona in an interface is an offer, not acceptance. Work remains visibly awaiting acceptance until a participant accepts responsibility for carrying the need toward delivery, waiting on a named condition, an honest block, decline, or handoff.

This **continuation responsibility** can be shared or partitioned. It does not make someone a compulsory leader, authorize assignment of peers, or determine everyone else's priorities. It ensures that incoming work-level questions and uncovered requirements have an accountable path.

Every adopted required outcome must show its accepted owner or ownership gap, dependencies, expected evidence, review requirement, and current disposition. Finishing all listed items does not prove the list covered the original need. Substantial work needs a separate scope-coverage check for omissions.

## Questions and conditional assumptions

A material unknown records the question, why it matters, affected claims, needed evidence, accepted follow-up or ownership gap, and whether limited exploration is permitted.

An assumption may be proposed, authorized for exploration, confirmed by appropriate evidence, contradicted, or withdrawn. These are different states. Approval to try a synthetic scenario never confirms a real-world fact.

A conditional result must state its conditions where the claim appears. When an assumption changes, dependent results and reviews need an applicability check. Asking questions should reduce consequential uncertainty, not become an endless intake process when a useful bounded response is already possible.

## Three complementary views of cooperation

**Individual agendas** describe what each persona considers worth attention. **The shared opportunity and obligation board** contains attributed proposals, questions, observations, findings, and offers. It has no hidden universal ranking. **Collective commitments** record responsibilities actually accepted and the dependencies between them.

Some commitments must precede others; independent commitments may proceed together. This partial order does not require a central semantic planner. Mechanical scheduling can allocate fair execution capacity without deciding the best domain method.

Personas may consider urgency, uncertainty, user importance, reversibility, risk, dependency impact, effort, and coordination overhead. These are considerations for judgment, not a compulsory numerical score. A concise decision summary records the chosen contribution and what would warrant reconsideration; it is not a request for private chain-of-thought.

## Commitment lifecycle

| State | Meaning | Required boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Offered | Specific terms have been proposed to a participant. | No responsibility is attributed as accepted yet. |
| Accepted | The participant accepts the exact outcome and terms. | Inputs, criteria, resources, and closure conditions are identified at the appropriate depth. |
| Working | Authorized action is being undertaken. | Running actions retain their actual status. |
| Blocked | A named condition prevents progress. | Ownership remains visible; the next decision or missing input is stated. |
| Submitted | An exact candidate is offered for assessment. | Submission is not approval. |
| Closed | The agreed disposition has been reached. | Closure identifies the exact result or other accepted end. |
| Canceled or handed off | Authority ends the obligation or a receiver accepts its transfer. | Silence, timeout, or nomination of a replacement does not complete a handoff. |

One persona may accept several responsibilities. Joint work may have several commitments for one outcome. A team does not need a contribution from every member to every task. Review is itself offered, accepted, funded, performed, and closed—not merely requested.

## Working agreements and disagreement

An agreement identifies its scope, exact terms, actual endorsements, objections, permitted variation, and revision or exit rules. New members can accept, question, or decline it. Agreements do not create authority, resources, factual truth, or unanimous belief.

Temporary coordination may be delegated with a defined scope and expiry. It does not establish a superior class of persona. Directional relationship memory may inform whom a persona consults, but reputation does not remove the need for claim-specific evidence.

| Conflict | Useful response |
|---|---|
| Factual disagreement | Compare sources, reproduce a check, or run a discriminating investigation. |
| Human preference disagreement | Ask the relevant decision-maker or use an already adopted preference rule. |
| Resource conflict | Negotiate within allocations or seek a decision from the controlling authority. |
| Ownership gap | Volunteer, renegotiate, recruit, request help, or show the gap honestly. |
| Permission conflict | Respect the controlling boundary; group discussion cannot waive it. |
| Dependency deadlock | Reconsider dependencies and, where permitted, use bounded provisional work. |

Independent, reversible alternatives may proceed in separate workspaces. Do not force disagreement for theatrical diversity, suppress dissent into “the team thinks,” or treat majority agreement as proof of a calculation.

## Consequential feedback must reach a disposition

A blocking finding identifies its exact subject, evidence, significance, accountable commitment or ownership gap, and the adopted rule for resolving it. Delivery, reading, acknowledgment, and resolution are separate events.

The responsible participant can accept a repair, dispute the finding with evidence, seek authorized deferral where the policy permits it, or escalate. A mandatory criterion cannot be waived by a polite reply. The finding survives summaries, history pagination, and context compaction until its required disposition is recorded.

Nonblocking suggestions remain optional. The supporting system enforces the status of known findings; it does not determine the truth of every specialist opinion.

## Integration and provisional iteration

Outputs that must fit together need shared interpretation of identifiers, units, coordinates, terminology, formats, ownership boundaries, and source versions as applicable. An interface agreement is ordinary work content, not a domain-specific engine.

A dependency must distinguish a final accepted input from a declared provisional input. For example, structural zones and service routes may initially depend on each other. Participants may agree on temporary envelopes, a shared baseline, ownership, compatibility checks, resource limits, and a stopping or escalation condition.

Each attempt preserves its conditions and remaining incompatibilities. The team may continue, change method, obtain help, or report non-convergence. Activity alone does not prove convergence. Final acceptance still requires current, compatible outputs.

Independent drafts remain separate until adopted. Adoption checks the prior version the decision actually considered; it does not silently overwrite another participant's accepted work. Coupled changes must become visible as one coherent assembly rather than a half-updated result.

## Improvement must compete honestly with finishing

A proposal identifies the motivating observation, affected outcome, expected benefit, possible regressions, uncertainty, smallest informative comparison, allowance, adoption authority, preserved baseline, and stop condition.

An actual comparison may support adoption, rejection, or deferral. Writing an attractive proposal is not achieved improvement. Optional refinement cannot indefinitely displace required completion or consume protected review resources without authorized reallocation.

A convincing cooperation trace links a peer's finding to an accepted response, an actual change in the result, and an appropriate check. Message volume, agreement, and a large team do not establish that trace.
