# 04 — Project

A project is a durable authenticated scope for shared task events, membership,
workspace bytes, artifacts, communications, resources, and lineage. It is not a
fixed milestone plan, role hierarchy, phase machine, acceptance pathway, or
team workflow.

## 1. Project identity and authority

A project record binds exact project/environment/principal identities,
authenticated intent bytes, access and membership policy, workspace root,
resource authority, zero or more unranked `domain_refs`, lifecycle, and
signatures.

The kernel validates those mechanics. It does not derive milestones, roles,
deliverables, reviewers, professions, tools, phases, schedules, or completion
from project text.

## 2. Open project records

Personas may author bounded open records for plans, questions, milestones,
dependencies, obligations, reviews, disagreements, calculations, schedules,
decisions, risks, and any other useful project concept. These records retain
exact authorship, scope, causal/evidence references, and supersession.

Their keys and labels have no substrate workflow semantics. A record named
“milestone,” “approved,” “blocked,” “review,” or “complete” cannot assign
authority, schedule a wake, gate another member, or complete the project.

## 3. Membership and plural agency

Membership is exact signed consent. Every task-ingress and resource-resume event
fans out unchanged to all active project-environment members. Each member has an
independent carrier and may act or not act.

The project does not require or elect a lead, owner, integrator, reviewer,
specialist, successor, or minimum team. Personas may communicate, invite,
transfer knowledge, review, author population actions, or continue alone.

## 4. Workspace and artifacts

Every persona worktree is isolated and leased through byte capture,
publication, and settlement. Shared publications preserve exact author, action,
entry revision, byte delta, and merge/conflict provenance.

Artifacts bind explicit signed MIME, content hash, byte length, role,
owner/scope, provenance, and plural `domain_refs`. Project item names,
filenames, extensions, directory names, and MIME alone do not assign artifact
meaning or quality.

Conflicts retain every exact alternative until an authorized persona-authored
resolution selects or synthesizes bytes.

## 5. Coordination and review

Messages, invitations, reviews, requests, publications, and schedules are
independent signed causal records. Several may coexist; the kernel does not
collapse them into a project phase or choose a next owner.

A review is an authored claim bound to evidence. Independent-review authority,
where exact principal intent requires it, must be established by exact reviewer
identity/membership and the declared acceptance mechanism—not a filename, role,
or self-description.

## 6. Capabilities and knowledge

Project wakes expose complete bounded unranked inventories of authorized
actions, local execution, mounted tools, visible memories/knowledge, peer
persona-knowledge metadata, and population facts. Personas choose what to
inspect, share by exact ref, access under authority, acquire/provision/invoke as
a tool, author, or ignore.

The project layer does not map task/domain/project words to tools, skills,
profession roles, artifacts, or team births. Perceived gap meaning is optional
opaque knowledge content with no dedicated lifecycle and does not gate progress
or completion.

## 7. Physical and external effects

An external or physical action requires the exact descriptor, consent,
attestation, cosign, budget/payment, and safety authority declared for that
effect. Project prose cannot supply or waive it.

Digital artifacts describing a physical design remain digital bytes. Accepting
a design does not itself authorize fabrication, payment, custody transfer, or
physical-state advancement.

## 8. Resource pause, resume, and quiescence

Budget exhaustion preserves exact pending carriers and best-so-far bytes. A
later signed resource event resumes the same project/task and fans out unchanged
to all active members.

No pending causal delivery means quiescent. Quiescence is nonterminal and does
not imply project completion, team agreement, abandonment, or inability to
improve.

## 9. Acceptance and lifecycle

Project acceptance comes only from exact authenticated principal acceptance or
an explicitly authorized verifier bound to current exact evidence. Work notes,
project-record labels, reviews, votes, tool success, artifact counts, scores,
and unchanged bytes do not complete the project.

Pause, cancellation, archival, transfer, and termination likewise require
their exact authenticated lifecycle authority. A persona claim cannot silently
change project lifecycle.

## 10. Discovery and sharing

Project/artifact cards expose only visibility-authorized exact signed metadata.
Discovery is not read/write authority. Fetch verifies signatures, policy,
expiry, content hash, byte length, MIME, and current revocation.

External content locators distribute exact integrity-anchored references, not
credentials. Provider or replica bytes are accepted only when they match the
signed binding.

## 11. Removed compatibility surface

There is no live compatibility for fixed ProjectMember roles, ProjectMilestone
or ProjectPhaseState reducers, LeadHandoffCeremony, task-class/acceptance-
pathway routing, contribution-credit fitness, verifier cascades, candidate
tables, round barriers, or project-completion inference from structured work
state. Historical records may remain opaque audit bytes but confer no current
workflow authority.

## 12. Design criteria

1. Projects preserve exact intent, membership, resources, workspace, artifacts,
   and lineage.
2. Project organization and records are persona-authored open material.
3. All active members receive exact task/resource events.
4. Workspace and artifact provenance remain attributable.
5. Capabilities and coordination are persona-navigated without host selectors.
6. Acceptance and lifecycle require exact authority.
7. Quiescence is nonterminal.
